# Myanmar Rice Research Intelligence — Analytical Report
Built 2026-10-09. Companion to the interactive app `myanmar-rice-research-intelligence.html` and the structured data in `data/`. Bracketed IDs ([S302], [D04], [P04]…) point to records in the app and in `data/sources.json`.

> Scope: Myanmar rice agriculture 2000–2026, with benchmark evidence from Thailand, Vietnam, Bangladesh, India, China, Cambodia and Laos. This is a documented, partial sample of the literature, not "all Myanmar rice research" (see §17).

## 1. Executive intelligence brief
- **Corpus:** 319 research records (Crossref + CGIAR repository + targeted search), 19 evidence-verified problems, 36 datasets with checked access links, 22 Myanmar interventions audited A–E, 14 neighbour-country cases, 12 research gaps, 17 scored project candidates, 1,558 typed relationships.
- **Productivity is stagnant and now falling.** USDA PSD rough-rice yield was 3.1 t/ha in 2000 and 2.71 t/ha in 2024 (Vietnam 6.12 t/ha) [D01]. National monsoon yields fell 8% in 2025 vs 2023 [S302]; exploitable yield gaps are relatively large in Myanmar [S189].
- **The binding problem today is economic, not agronomic alone.** Between the 2023 and 2025 monsoons input spending per acre rose 63%, wages 83–88%, plowing 55%, urea 23%, while farmgate paddy fell 6% and profit per acre fell 40% to a six-year low [S302]. Fuel and urea prices jumped again in mid-2026 [S304]; the dual exchange rate acted as a ~24% export tax [S239].
- **Conflict changed the production function.** Fatal violence cut rice TFP ~4% [S200]; it raised fertilizer prices, lowered use and lowered fertilizer use efficiency each year since 2021 [S269]; formal credit collapsed in conflict areas [S286].
- **Labour scarcity is reshaping practice.** ~50% (monsoon) and ~75% (dry season) of farmers now direct-seed, mostly by broadcasting, because of labour and water constraints, with a perceived yield penalty [S301]. Market-driven mechanization scaled 2011–2020 [S162] but combine use fell 12 pp after 2020 [S252].
- **Research-to-practice gap:** of 22 audited interventions, 3 reached scale (A), 3 partial (B), 12 stalled at pilot (C), 3 never left the proposal stage (D), 1 unknown (E). Weak or non-random evaluation is the most common barrier (13 interventions).
- **#1 recommended project (PR01, score 16.45/25):** Price-aware nitrogen advice for monsoon rice, delivered through existing digital advisory channels. It closes a documented implementation gap (Myanmar's Rice Crop Manager stopped at a 2019 prototype [S112] while comparable advice raised yields 17–19% in India [S267]) using open panel data [D04] and an existing delivery channel that reached 139,000 farmers in 2025 [S298].

**P02 Cost-price squeeze: rising input costs, falling farm profitability.** Symptom: Falling profit per acre; reduced input use. Immediate: Higher wages, mechanization fees, fertilizer and fuel prices; Lower farmgate paddy prices tracking world prices. Root: Dual exchange-rate regime and import restrictions; Conflict-related transport and market disruption; Global fuel and fertilizer shocks.
  - Between the 2023 and 2025 monsoons: urea +23%, plowing costs +55%, wages +88% (men) and +83% (women), input expenditure per acre +63%, farmgate paddy -6%, nominal profit per acre -40% (lowest in six years). [S302]
  - Real paddy profits in the 2024 dry season fell 15% versus 2023 after a 41% rise the year before - profitability is volatile. [S253], [S217]
  - The dual exchange-rate system acted as an across-the-board export tax of about 24% (Aug 2022-Aug 2024) and raised fertilizer prices ~10% relative to Thailand. [S239]
  - By end-June 2026 diesel was 34% and petrol 57% higher than end-February; urea reference prices were 28-49% above the previous monsoon. [S304]

**P04 Fertilizer cost, access and declining use efficiency.** Symptom: Lower or mistimed fertilizer use; weak yield response. Immediate: High urea prices relative to paddy; Reduced access to extension and optimal blends; Poor timing vs recommendations. Root: FX and import licensing restrictions; Conflict; Absence of site- and price-specific recommendations.
  - Greater intensity of violent events is associated with higher fertilizer prices (especially far from import points), lower use, lower rice yield response at given N, and a decline in fertilizer use efficiency each year since the conflict began. [S269]
  - Monsoon fertilizer use fell about 20% across 2020-2022 monsoons. [S255]
  - In the 2022 dry season fertilizer use fell only 6% despite a 51% price increase. [S184]
  - Fertilizer use on rice rose 20% in the 2023 monsoon as urea prices fell 16%, contributing to a 7% yield gain. [S252]

**P03 Rural labour scarcity and rising agricultural wages.** Symptom: Higher wage bill; labour unavailable at peak operations. Immediate: Migration to cities and abroad; Insecurity limiting worker mobility. Root: Structural transformation since 2011; Post-2021 conflict and economic crisis.
  - Since 2011 farmers faced labour shortages and rising wages as workers moved to urban sectors; they responded by mechanizing rapidly. [S132], [S162]
  - Farm wages rose 88% (men) and 83% (women) between the 2023 and 2025 monsoons. [S302]
  - Farmers in the most insecure areas were 7-15 percentage points more likely to report lacking access to agricultural labourers. [S239]
  - Labour shortages and water constraints are the primary reasons for adopting direct-seeded rice. [S301]

**P05 Flood, cyclone and heavy-rain losses in Delta and coastal rice.** Symptom: Seasonal yield losses and crop failure. Immediate: Riverine and pluvial flooding; Cyclones and storm surge. Root: Low-lying delta topography; Climate variability and change; Limited drainage and early warning.
  - In the 2025 monsoon, 10% of paddy farmers reported floods (yields -34% when affected) and 15% heavy rains (-15%). [S302]
  - In the 2023 monsoon floods affected 7% of farmers with 32% yield losses. [S252]
  - Cyclone Mocha affected 13% of crop farmers; 3% lost their whole dry-season harvest; coastal yields fell 29%. [S217]
  - Of 1,046 cyclone events (1970-2018) in four countries, 138 were associated with recorded rice damage; agricultural losses are under-reported in disaster databases. [S168]

## 4. 2000–2026 research landscape
Myanmar records by period: 2000–2004: 3; 2005–2009: 7; 2010–2014: 26; 2015–2019: 81; 2020–2024: 140; 2025–2029: 46.
Records per problem (curated + auto-tagged): Persistent yield gap and stagnant paddy productivity 91; Cost-price squeeze: rising input costs, falling farm profitability 88; Price distortions and weak price transmission 80; Extension and information gap 72; Weak and shock-sensitive access to quality seed 64; Fertilizer cost, access and declining use efficiency 63; Rural labour scarcity and rising agricultural wages 53; Mechanization service access and rising service costs 51.
Most common methods: Survey (114); Qualitative / review (41); Regression / econometrics (39); Economic / cost-benefit analysis (37); Crop / hydrological simulation (32); GIS / spatial (29); Remote sensing (23); Time series (14). Randomized or quasi-experimental designs are rare (3 records); optimization/OR is thin (10).
Source tiers: Tier 2 (FAO/IRRI/CGIAR/IFPRI/World Bank…) 128, Tier 3 (peer-reviewed/university) 190, Tier 4 1. Confidence: High 199, Medium 20, Low 100. Since 2021 the evidence base is dominated by IFPRI phone-panel work; independent academic field research inside Myanmar has thinned.

## 5. Research evidence database
`data/studies.json` holds all 319 records with objective, methodology, findings, effect sizes, problems, datasets, interventions, limitations, tier, confidence and verification status. Curated records (89) were written from abstracts/summaries read for this build; others carry auto-extracted objective and finding sentences (labelled as such). Key curated studies:

- [S302] Aung, Zin Wai, Minten, Bart (2026). Rice productivity and profitability in Myanmar: Assessment of the 2025 monsoon season. Myanmar SSP Working Paper. — *Assess 2025 monsoon paddy productivity and profitability versus 2023 using the MAPS phone panel.* Findings: National yields -8% vs 2023, driven by fewer labour inputs, more broadcasting and more floods/heavy rain; Coastal zone -22%, Delta -7%; Input expenditure per acre +63%; urea +23%; plowing +55%; wages +83-88%; farmgate paddy -6%
- [S252] Myanmar Agriculture Policy Support Activity (2024). Rice productivity and profitability in Myanmar: Assessment of the 2023 monsoon. IFPRI Myanmar SSP Working Paper. — *Assess 2023 monsoon rice productivity and profitability.* Findings: National yields +7% vs 2022 from more fertilizer, more transplanting and fewer droughts; Urea price -16%; mechanization costs +42%; Fertilizer use +20%
- [S269] Takeshima, Hiroyuki, Minten, Bart, van Asselt, Joanna et al. (2025). Fertilizer and conflicts: Evidence from Myanmar. Formally Published. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodpol.2024.102786 — *Study how conflict plus global fertilizer shocks affected fertilizer prices, use, yield response and efficiency.* Findings: More violent events → higher fertilizer prices, especially far from import points; Lower likelihood and quantity of fertilizer use; Lower rice yield response at given N
- [S200] Myanmar Agriculture Policy Support Activity (2023). Conflict and agricultural productivity: Evidence from Myanmar. International Food Policy Research Institute. https://doi.org/10.2499/p15738coll2.136600 — *Estimate how the post-2021 surge in violence affected rice total factor productivity and technology use.* Findings: Increases in fatal violent events reduced rice TFP by ~4% on average; Poorer, less-capitalised farmers more affected (higher output elasticity to owned equipment); Partly due to reduced extension access; lower mechanization fees mitigate effects
- [S239] Myanmar Agrifood Program for Strategy and Analysis (2024). How have foreign exchange market distortions and conflict affected agricultural production incentives in Myanmar?. IFPRI Myanmar SSP Working Paper. — *Examine how FX distortions and conflict changed agricultural prices and production incentives.* Findings: Dual exchange rate acted as ~24% export tax (Aug 2022-Aug 2024); Fertilizer ~10% more expensive than in Thailand; Insecure areas: input prices +1-6%, input availability gaps +1-6 pp, labour access gaps +7-15 pp
- [S301] Goeb, Joseph, Minten, Bart, Synt, Nang Lun Kham et al. (2026). Rice establishment methods in Myanmar: Trends, perceptions, and constraints. Myanmar SSP Research Note. — *Document trends, perceptions and constraints in rice establishment methods.* Findings: DSR used by ~50% of farmers in monsoon and ~75% in dry season, mostly broadcasting; Stable 2023-2025 despite shocks; Higher DSR among larger and non-irrigated farms

International Food Policy Research Institute. https://doi.org/10.2499/p15738coll2.136706 — *Test how mechanization fees affect the inverse productivity-plot size relationship.* Findings: Small plots ~30% more productive than large plots; Rising mechanization fees, especially in conflict townships, attenuated this relationship

## 12. Gap analysis
- **G02 [Implementation] Proven nutrient decision tools never reached Myanmar farmers** — Site-specific nutrient advice raised yields 17-19% in India (S267) and integrated recommendations scaled to 20.9 million farmers in China (S076); a Myanmar Rice Crop Manager prototype (2019) has no deployment record. Evidence: [S112], [S267], [S076]. Priority High.
- **G05 [Implementation] Quality seed does not reach farmers after shocks** — Off-farm seed raises yields, but its use falls after shocks except near early-generation seed farms. Evidence: [S299], [S252]. Priority Medium.
- **G11 [Implementation] No randomized evidence on digital advisory effects for rice** — Current DCAS evidence is non-random and implementer-affiliated (S262). Evidence: [S262], [S259], [S274]. Priority High.
- **G01 [Knowledge] No representative post-2020 estimate of harvest and post-harvest losses** — Loss evidence comes from field experiments and two-township rodent studies; national loss rates under current mechanization are unknown. Evidence: [S119], [S052]. Priority Medium.
- **G08 [Knowledge] Economic-optimum N rates under current prices are not estimated** — Yield response to N is weakening under conflict (S269); recommendations ignore the urea:paddy price ratio and establishment method. Evidence: [S269], [S302], [S158], [S090]. Priority High.
- **G10 [Knowledge] Farmgate price transmission under the dual FX regime is not monitored continuously** — FX policy hurt incentives more than conflict (S239), but no routine monitor exists. Evidence: [S239]. Priority Medium.
- **G12 [Knowledge] Effect of mechanization cost spikes on timeliness losses unquantified** — Fees rose 42-59%; consequences for harvest timing and losses are not measured. Evidence: [S252], [S253], [S302]. Priority Medium.
- **G04 [Data] Official subnational production statistics not public after 2020** — Analysts rely on phone panels and satellites; disagreement between sources cannot be adjudicated. Evidence: [S251]. Priority High.
- **G09 [Data] Coastal salinity and elevation data are thin or inconsistent** — Satellite salinity models lack in-situ EC; elevation datasets disagree (S195). Evidence: [S155], [S195]. Priority Medium.
- **G07 [Technology] Precision/row seeding services absent while broadcasting spreads** — Labour scarcity drives broadcast DSR with a perceived yield penalty; Vietnam's mechanized seeding shows an alternative. Evidence: [S301], [S309]. Priority High.
- **G03 [Decision] No operational township-level flood/drought loss monitoring** — Relief, seed replacement and anticipatory decisions lack a near-real-time link between satellite hazard data and farm yield losses. Evidence: [S302], [S252], [S072], [S060]. Priority High.
- **G06 [Decision] Season-ahead profitability is invisible to farmers and policymakers** — Profit swings of +41% to -40% between seasons are only documented after the fact. Evidence: [S217], [S253], [S302]. Priority Medium.

## 14. Top-5 project designs
### 1. PR01 — Price-aware nitrogen advice for monsoon rice, delivered through existing digital advisory channels (score 16.45)
- **User → decision → information → analysis → action → outcome:** Rice farmers (via advisory app/extension); advisory providers → How much urea to apply, and when, given this season's urea and paddy prices, zone and establishment method → Plot yield response to N by zone and establishment method; current urea and expected paddy prices; shock risk → Yield-response functions (quadratic-plateau) estimated on MAPS panel with household fixed effects and weather/conflict controls → economic optimum N at price ratio → simple lookup table → Advisory recommends a rate band and split timing each season → Higher profit per acre and N use efficiency; avoided losses from over/under-application
- **Why it matters:** Fertilizer is the single most important commercial input. Prices rose 23% (2023-2025) and another 28-49% in 2026, profits fell 40%, and conflict has eroded fertilizer use efficiency every year since 2021. A better rate decision touches nearly every rice farmer, every season.
- **Research question:** What nitrogen rate (and split) maximizes expected profit per acre for monsoon rice by agro-ecological zone and establishment method at current urea:paddy price ratios, and does delivering this advice raise farmer profit?
- **Hypothesis:** H1: Yield response to N differs by zone, establishment method and conflict exposure, so a single national recommendation is mis-specified. H2: Price-aware advice raises profit per acre by reducing N where marginal returns are below cost and increasing it where they exceed cost.
- **Target population/location:** Monsoon rice farmers in Delta, Dry Zone and Coastal zones (MAPS sample covers all states/regions)
- **Dependent variable:** Yield (t/ha; converted from baskets/acre using the paddy basket weight documented by MAPS) and profit per acre
- **Baseline method:** Descriptive N-yield curves by zone; pooled OLS quadratic response; economic optimum = where marginal product x paddy price = urea price
- **Advanced method (only if justified):** Panel fixed effects / correlated random effects with control function for N endogeneity (as in S269); justified because N is chosen by farmers. ML (e.g., causal forests) only if heterogeneity remains unexplained.
- **Validation:** Leave-one-round-out prediction of yields; comparison with agronomic benchmarks (S090, S114); then a randomized A/B test of advice variants inside the advisory app (outcome: crop-cut yield, N use, profit).
- **Prediction vs causation:** Observational response curves are associations (better farmers apply more N). The A/B test provides causal evidence on the advice itself.
- **Expected output:** Seasonal lookup table (zone x establishment x price ratio → N band and timing) + open notebook + 4-page brief
- **Intervention pathway:** Deliver as SMS/app advisory through an existing digital climate-advisory provider (I04) and DoA extension
- **End user:** Rice farmers; advisory providers; DoA extension
- **Impact measurement:** Change in profit per acre, N use efficiency (kg grain/kg N) and adoption rate among advised vs control users
- **Required data:** Plot N, P, K quantities and timing; Plot yield (largest plot) and area; Urea and paddy prices; Establishment method; Seed source; Shocks (flood, drought, pests); Township; conflict events; Rainfall
- **Available data:** D04 MAPS rounds 1-8 (inputs, yields, prices, establishment, shocks); D13 CHIRPS rainfall; D18 ACLED; D17 SoilGrids (coarse); D06 retail rice prices (no Delta coverage)
- **Missing data:** Fertilizer timing detail may be limited in MAPS (verify questionnaire); Field-measured yields (crop cuts) for validation; Soil N supply
- **Independent variables:** N rate (kg/ha) and its square; Urea:paddy price ratio; Establishment method; Zone
- **Confounders:** Farmer skill/fixed effects; Soil quality; Irrigation; Seed source; Weather shocks; Conflict exposure; Mechanization access
- **Limitations:** MAPS yields are self-reported for the largest plot; Soil N supply unobserved; Advice cannot fix fertilizer availability in cut-off areas (Rakhine)
- **Risks:** Partner access; Security for crop cuts; Price volatility within season
- **Evidence/gaps:** problems [P04], [P02], [P14], [P01]; gaps [G02], [G08]; datasets [D04], [D13], [D18], [D17], [D06]; benchmarks [B01], [B08], [B02]

### 2. PR04 — Crop-establishment decision model: broadcast vs row-seeding vs transplanting under labour and water constraints (score 14.72)
- **User → decision → information → analysis → action → outcome:** Farmers, service providers, advisory platforms → Which establishment method maximizes expected profit given wage, seed, water and service availability → Yield and cost by method, zone and season; wage and fee levels → Endogenous-switching or matching estimates of method yield/profit gaps; break-even wage thresholds; service-demand map → Advise method by zone; identify where row-seeding services pay off → Recover part of perceived broadcast yield penalty while saving labour
- **Why it matters:** Half of monsoon farmers and three-quarters of dry-season farmers now broadcast seed because labour and water are scarce; farmers believe this costs ~11 baskets/acre and 2025 yields fell as broadcasting rose.
- **Research question:** Under which wage, water and service conditions does broadcasting, mechanized row/drum seeding or transplanting maximize profit?
- **Hypothesis:** Broadcast DSR is profit-maximizing only above a wage threshold; precision seeding dominates where services exist.
- **Target population/location:** Monsoon and dry-season rice farmers nationally
- **Dependent variable:** Profit per acre; yield
- **Baseline method:** Partial budgets by method and zone
- **Advanced method (only if justified):** Endogenous switching regression or matching to address self-selection
- **Validation:** Out-of-sample by round; field demonstration plots
- **Prediction vs causation:** Method choice is self-selected; estimates are conditional associations unless trials are run.
- **Expected output:** Break-even wage maps and advisory rules
- **Intervention pathway:** Promote row/drum seeding services via existing outsourcing providers (I01)
- **End user:** Farmers; machinery service providers
- **Impact measurement:** Yield and profit vs broadcasting neighbours
- **Required data:** Establishment method; Yields; Wages; Seed rate; Weed control cost; Irrigation
- **Available data:** D04 MAPS
- **Missing data:** Row/drum seeder availability; Measured yield by method
- **Independent variables:** Establishment method; Wage rate; Irrigation
- **Confounders:** Farm size; Land type; Skill; Flood exposure
- **Limitations:** Perceived vs measured yield gap
- **Risks:** Seeder supply
- **Evidence/gaps:** problems [P18], [P03], [P12], [P01]; gaps [G07]; datasets [D04], [D13], [D19]; benchmarks [B03]

## 15. #1 recommended project — the final question
*If I am a developing data analyst / data scientist and can do only one project with a realistic chance of helping Myanmar rice agriculture, what should I work on?*

**Work on price-aware nitrogen advice for monsoon rice, delivered through existing digital advisory channels (PR01).**

- **Why:** fertilizer is the largest commercial input; its economics deteriorated sharply (urea +23% and profits −40% between the 2023 and 2025 monsoons [S302]; urea +28–49% again in mid-2026 [S304]); conflict lowered fertilizer use efficiency every year since 2021 [S269]. Doubling fertilizer is associated with only ~9% more yield on average [S302], so the right rate depends strongly on prices and conditions — exactly where a data analyst adds value. Comparable decision tools work: +17–19% yield in Odisha on-farm trials [S267]; +11% yield with −15–18% N for 20.9 million Chinese farmers [S076]. Myanmar's own Rice Crop Manager stalled at a 2019 prototype [S112].
- **What data:** MAPS rounds 1–8 [D04] (plot fertilizer, yields, prices, establishment method, shocks, township), CHIRPS [D13], ACLED [D18], SoilGrids [D17]; collect crop-cut yields and split-application dates in a pilot.
- **What method:** start with zone × establishment-method yield-response curves and the economic optimum where marginal product × paddy price = urea price; then panel fixed effects / correlated random effects with a control function for endogenous N (as in [S269]); machine learning only if heterogeneity remains unexplained. Validate out of sample by round, against agronomic benchmarks (40 kg N/ha on top-yield farms in [S090]; ORYZA potentials in [S114]), and with a randomized A/B test of advice variants.
- **What decision improves:** each farmer's N rate and timing per season, given the urea:paddy price ratio, zone and establishment method.
- **How it reaches farmers:** seasonal lookup tables delivered through the Htwet Toe digital advisory platform (139,000 farmers across 10 commodities in 2025 [S298]; rice pilot with 5,000 Delta farmers [S230]) and DoA extension; impact measured as profit per acre and kg grain per kg N against control users.
- **Honest limits:** MAPS yields are self-reported for the largest plot; soil N supply is unobserved; advice cannot fix fertilizer unavailability in cut-off areas such as Rakhine [S302]; DCAS impact claims so far are non-random [S262].
